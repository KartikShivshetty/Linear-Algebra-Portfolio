Dynamic Mapping Systems

A matrix functions as an active operator. Multiplying an incoming vector by a matrix modifies spatial coordinates and converts inputs into targeted outputs: $Ax=b$.

Operational Actions

Matrices rotate fields, scale objects, mirror axes, and stretch transformations across computer graphics landscapes.

Angular Rotation

Rotates target points about the origin through an angle $\theta$, useful in robotics mechanics and navigation tracking.

$$R(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$$

Shearing Deformation

Slants spatial bounds along a fixed axis by applying a multiplier $k$, foundational for text and shape deformation.

$$S_{horizontal} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & k \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

First-Order Equations

A linear function establishes relationship arrays where all variables have power one and sketch uniform paths through space.

$$ax + by = c$$

Simultaneous Constraints

Systems of linear equations cluster independent operational lines and require solving for variables that satisfy all statements synchronously.

Row Echelon Form

Zero rows are pushed downward and each leading pivot shifts progressively rightward with zeros beneath it.

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Reduced Row Echelon Form

Scales leading pivots to one and clears non-zero values both above and below each pivot.

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

04

Closure Under Addition

Combining any two vectors within the defined space boundary must result in another enclosed vector.

$$u + v \in V$$

Closure Under Scalar Scaling

Scaling an internal vector by any real scalar cannot move the product outside the vector domain boundary.

$$c \cdot v \in V$$

Column Space

The span generated across all vertical matrix columns, describing the full set of achievable outputs.

$$\text{Dim} = \text{Rank}(A)$$

Null Space

All incoming vectors that compress to the zero vector during transformation, representing data lost by the system.

$$\text{Dim} = n - \text{Rank}(A)$$

Rank-Nullity Balancing Theorem

The total input dimension splits into active processing directions and muted redundant lines.

$$\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Nullity}(A) = n$$

Dot Product Alignment

A zero scalar result implies complete directional independence with zero spatial overlap.

Euclidean Magnitude

Derives absolute distance in multidimensional coordinate fields through self-dot integration.

$$\|u\| = \sqrt{u \cdot u}$$

Linear Projection Scaling

Maps the component span of a vector onto the trajectory line specified by another target vector.

$$\text{proj}_u(v) = \left( \frac{v \cdot u}{u \cdot u} \right) u$$

Least Squares

Used when overdetermined structural data systems do not have exact intersecting solutions.

$$A^T A \hat{x} = A^T b$$

QR Factorization Architecture

$$A = Q \cdot R$$

Invariant Trajectories

During spatial transformations, certain pathways preserve orientation while stretching or compressing along the same line.

Eigenvalue Identity

Applying matrix $A$ to eigenvector $X$ yields the same result as scaling the vector by $\lambda$.

$$A X = \lambda X$$

Characteristic Equation

Extracts active stretching scalar coefficients by solving a determinant polynomial.

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$$

Matrix Diagonalization

Transforms coupled maps into an independent diagonal framework built from distinct eigenvalues.

$$A = P D P^{-1} \implies A^n = P D^n P^{-1}$$

Mathematical matrix decompositions

Compression method

Compression is achieved by keeping only the most meaningful singular values and discarding minor components according to the low-rank approximation principle.

Singular Value Factorization

SVD breaks down signal matrix $A$ into left and right singular vectors, matching orthonormal bases spanning the column and row spaces.

$$A = U \Sigma V^T$$

Low-Rank Approximation

Compression keeps the top $k=20$ singular values and reconstructs the compressed signal matrix.

$$A_k = U_k \Sigma_k V_k^T$$
